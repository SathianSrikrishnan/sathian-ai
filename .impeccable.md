## Design Context

### Users

**Primary: Parents (25–45)**
They arrive from an Instagram ad or a friend's recommendation. Their child has a loose tooth or just lost one. They're not searching for this — it finds them. The landing page has seconds to earn trust and communicate "this is a ritual you do with your child." If it feels like a crypto experiment or a tech product, they bounce. If it feels like something a thoughtful friend recommended — a ritual worth trying — they stay. Parents are having children later; this skews older than typical kid-tech audiences.

**Secondary: Children (4.5–9, peak 5–7)**
Children start losing teeth around 4.5 and are done by 8-9. The sweet spot is 5-7 — old enough to participate, young enough to believe in magic. The child view exists so kids experience the product *alongside* their parent. It's a ritual you do together. The child's experience should feel like opening a storybook, not using an app.

**Tertiary: Extended family ("the tribe")**
Grandma writes a note. Uncle adds savings. Dad sends a message. These people arrive via a shared link and need to understand what's happening in 5 seconds and contribute in 30.

### Brand Personality

**Three words: Mythic. Trustworthy. Hopeful.**

A fourth dimension: **Childlike** — not childish. The difference between a Miyazaki film and a cartoon. There's real craft, real depth, but it speaks to something innocent and earnest.

**Voice:** Warm, direct, unhurried. Sentences that a parent would actually say to their child. "Your child just lost a tooth. Let's make it the first thing they ever own." — that's the register. No crypto jargon. No startup speak. No "revolutionizing" anything.

**Emotional goal:** The parent should feel a small ache of tenderness — the same feeling as finding their child's baby shoes in a drawer. Then confidence that this is real, simple, and permanent.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dual-view system:**
- **Parent view (default/landing):** Warm light. Cream and aged paper tones. Gold as the primary accent — not shiny/metallic gold, but warm amber-gold like candlelight. The feeling of a well-made children's book from a bookshop, not a tech product. Trust-building, unhurried, breathing space.
- **Child view (stories/in-app):** Deep night sky. Not pure black — deep indigo with warmth. Gold becomes more luminous here, like fireflies or starlight. This is the wonder mode. The feeling of being tucked in and told a story. Age-adaptive: simpler visuals for younger children, more narrative depth for older ones.

**Theme logic:**
- Marketing/landing pages → Light (parent trust)
- Story experience → Dark (child wonder)
- App/dashboard → Toggleable (parent manages in light, child explores in dark)

**Illustration style decision (CRITICAL for scale):**
The stories are the acquisition funnel — 50–70 cultural traditions, shared for free, bringing people to the site via social. Each story needs consistent characters that can eventually live across the whole site (hero images, videos, animations). The style must be:
- Producible at scale via AI workflows (Gemini, etc.) with character consistency
- Warm and animated-feeling — Ghibli-inspired softness, not Pixar photorealism (too hard to produce consistently)
- Distinctive enough that "Tooth Fairy Network characters" become recognizable across stories
- Age-appropriate for 5-7 year olds but not embarrassing for a 9-year-old

**References:**
- Studio Ghibli — mythic but accessible, soft illustration quality
- Rifle Paper Co. — warm, textured, handmade-feeling print quality
- Stripe's clarity and trust signals applied to a children's product
- The warmth of a Montessori classroom — natural materials, not plastic

**Anti-references:**
- Crypto/DeFi aesthetic (dark + neon + glassmorphism)
- Generic SaaS landing pages (gradient hero, three feature cards, testimonials)
- "Kid-tech" with rounded everything and primary colors
- Pixar-level photorealism (can't maintain consistency at scale)
- Any interface that looks like it was made by AI in 2024

**Color direction:**
- Parent palette: Cream base (~oklch(97% 0.01 80)), warm brown text (~oklch(30% 0.03 70)), amber-gold accent (~oklch(75% 0.15 75)). Neutrals tinted toward amber, never cool gray.
- Child palette: Deep indigo base (~oklch(15% 0.04 270)), soft warm white text (~oklch(95% 0.01 80)), luminous gold accent (~oklch(82% 0.17 85)), stardust teal as secondary (~oklch(78% 0.12 185)). No pure black. No pure white.
- Both palettes share gold as the throughline — it's the brand color, the thread that connects parent trust and child wonder.

### Design Principles

1. **Earned trust, not asserted trust.** Don't say "secure" or "permanent" with badges and shields. Show it through the quality of the craft. A beautiful, unhurried page earns more trust than a security icon.

2. **The story IS the product.** Every culture has a tooth fairy myth. The stories aren't content marketing — they're the acquisition funnel AND the competitive moat. Shared for free on social, they bring people to the site. Characters from the stories should eventually live across the whole frontend — hero images, animations, the landing page. Design them like they matter, because they do. 50–70 cultural stories, each its own small world.

3. **Two modes, one thread.** Parent view and child view are different emotional registers of the same product. Gold connects them. The transition between modes should feel like turning a page, not switching apps.

4. **Simple feature, deep world.** The product is simple: lose a tooth → create a keepsake → family contributes → child owns it forever. The depth comes from the universe around it — the myths, the characters, the cultural richness. Design should make the simple feel significant.

5. **Made by hand, kept by machine.** The aesthetic should feel handcrafted — warm textures, considered typography, illustration over photography where possible. But the underlying technology (Solana, cNFTs, escrow) should be invisible. The child never sees a wallet address. The parent barely does.

### Technical Constraints

- **Framework:** Next.js 14+ (App Router), deployed on Vercel
- **Animation:** Framer Motion (already in use)
- **Styling:** Tailwind CSS utilities + inline styles (no CSS-in-JS library)
- **Fonts:** Must replace current stack (Playfair Display, Lora, Plus Jakarta Sans — all banned). New fonts loaded via `next/font/google`.
- **Images:** Mix of generated illustrations and photography. Hero images needed for landing page.
- **Responsive:** Mobile-first. Parents will pull this up on their phone at bedtime.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA minimum. Children and grandparents both use this.
