# Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee

Status: storyboard lock draft
Date: 2026-05-07

## Job

This is Story 2 of the homepage trilogy. It is the mythic origin: how Tanda's first tooth fee became a promise, why her magic is tied to mercy, and why she can carry what children outgrow without letting it disappear.

The story should feel like a bedtime story first. The product meaning is underneath it: the tooth is still the child's, the adult is entrusted with it, and the added memory becomes the first Toothlight.

## Production Direction

Target length: 27 story frames plus CTA.

Visual direction: current soft 3D cinematic character quality, using the existing Tanda and father references as the character baseline. Pull only the restraint, warmth, and handmade texture from the older watercolor art. Do not switch the whole story back into watercolor.

Image format: portrait storybook frames, 9:16 or close to the current 576x1024 / 768x1344 site ratio. Generate at 2K, crop-safe for mobile full-screen viewing.

Folder target: `public/story-assets/viking-origin/`.

Story data target: `src/data/stories/viking-origin.ts`.

Recommended site title: **Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee**

Recommended subtitle: **A Viking story of ships, mercy, and the first Toothlight.**

## Character Locks

### Young Tanda

Age seven. Human child at first, no wings until the origin moment. Warm brown eyes, chestnut-brown wavy hair that visually connects to adult Tanda, practical wool dress in rust and cream, scuffed boots or bare feet depending on scene, one front tooth missing after the loss. Curious, stubborn, funny, emotionally direct.

Key expression range: proud helper, irritated child, secretly worried, gap-toothed delight, solemn promise.

### Tanda's Father

Shipbuilder first, voyager second, fighter only when forced. Broad build, kind tired eyes, rope-callused hands, brown leather work vest over cream wool, simple belt, shipwright tools, no horned helmet. He is not a perfect saint. He gets tired, he is under pressure, and his mercy costs him something.

Key visual anchor: he kneels to children's eye level. His hands are large but careful.

### The Boy Across the Water

Seven or eight. From another northern shore. Practical clothes, frightened but not pitiful. He is not a lesson object; he wants to protect his sister and repair the family's small boat.

### Props

The first tooth. A small coin for the tooth fee. Sailcloth wrapping. A tiny carved oak tooth-keeper made by Tanda's father, shaped like the curved rib of a ship and carried inside his vest near his heart. Wooden tooth box carved by Tanda's father. Ship ribs. Pine pitch. Rope coil. Cracked small boat. A goat used once for comedy.

## Storyboard

### 01. Cover

Image beat: Young Tanda on a cold shoreline beside the ribs of a half-built ship. Her father works in the background. The sea is slate blue, the shipyard glows with hearth amber.

Read-aloud copy:

**Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee**

A Viking story of ships, mercy, and the first Toothlight.

Production note: This should establish the whole visual grammar: handmade world, soft 3D faces, not costume-pageantry.

### 02. Ships Grow From Oak

Image beat: Wide view of a Viking-age shipyard. Curved oak ribs rise from the beach like a sleeping animal's bones. Tanda is small in the frame, ducking under a plank.

Read-aloud copy:

Long before Tanda had wings, she lived where the sea slapped the stones and ships grew rib by rib from oak.

She was seven, or almost seven, depending on who was asking.

### 03. The Maker

Image beat: Her father shapes a plank with a hand plane. Curling wood shavings fall around Tanda's feet.

Read-aloud copy:

Her father built ships.

He could tap a board and know if it wanted to be a keel, an oar, or firewood.

Tanda thought this was unfair. The boards never answered her when she tapped them.

### 04. Tanda Helps

Image beat: Tanda proudly holds a plank in place while her sleeve gets stuck to pine pitch. Her father notices too late.

Read-aloud copy:

Tanda wanted to help.

She pressed one plank exactly where he told her, then discovered her sleeve had joined the ship.

"The ship likes me," she said.

"The ship has poor judgment," said her father.

### 05. The Wobble

Image beat: Close on Tanda touching her mouth with suspicion while pretending to inspect tools.

Read-aloud copy:

That morning, a tooth had been wobbling in her mouth like a loose peg.

Tanda kept it secret.

Grown-ups became very interested in teeth, and interest was tiring.

### 06. The Point Comes Loose

Image beat: Supper by firelight. Tanda bites dried fish with heroic seriousness; the tooth pops into her palm.

Read-aloud copy:

At supper she bit a strip of dried fish to prove a point.

The point came loose.

So did the tooth.

### 07. A Small Rude Thing

Image beat: Tanda holds the tiny tooth in her palm, frowning at it. Her father sits near her, not reaching yet.

Read-aloud copy:

It sat in her palm, small and wet and rude.

Tanda did not feel older.

She felt as if a piece of herself had fallen out by mistake.

### 08. The Tooth Fee

Image beat: Her father washes the tooth carefully in a wooden bowl and places a small bright coin beside it.

Read-aloud copy:

Her father cleaned the tooth in warm water.

Then he set a bright coin beside it.

"A tooth fee," he said. "For the one who did the growing."

### 09. Still Mine

Image beat: Tanda covers the tooth with both hands. Her father leans in, amused and serious.

Read-aloud copy:

Tanda covered the tooth with both hands.

"If I take the coin, is it still mine?"

"Yes," her father said. "A fee is not a purchase. It is a promise to notice."

### 10. May I Carry It?

Image beat: Morning before departure. The ship is being readied behind them. Her father kneels and asks permission to carry the tooth. In his palm is a tiny carved oak tooth-keeper, shaped like the curved rib of a ship.

Read-aloud copy:

The next morning, the ship was leaving.

Her father asked, "May I carry your tooth until I come home?"

Tanda thought about this for a serious amount of time.

"You may carry it," she said, "but you may not lose it, trade it, or let the goat chew it."

### 11. The Goat Tries Anyway

Image beat: Goat noses at the sailcloth while father tries to wrap the tooth and fit it into the carved tooth-keeper. Tanda blocks the goat with great dignity.

Read-aloud copy:

The goat tried immediately.

Tanda stood guard while her father wrapped the tooth in sailcloth and tucked it into the little oak keeper.

His fingers were too big for the tiny latch.

Tanda fixed it and looked very patient about it.

### 12. The Ship Leaves

Image beat: Sail rising. Tanda stands on the shore, angry and small, with one fist closed around the coin.

Read-aloud copy:

When the sail rose, Tanda's throat went tight.

The ship she had helped build now did the one thing she hated.

It took him away.

### 13. What Promises Can Do

Image beat: Father at the rail looking back; Tanda on shore in the distance. One hand rests over the small tooth-keeper inside his vest.

Read-aloud copy:

"You promised," she had said.

"I promised to carry what is yours," he had answered.

"I cannot promise the sea will ask less of me."

### 14. Waiting Work

Image beat: Tanda in the shipyard after departure, sweeping wood shavings into little piles, keeping herself busy.

Read-aloud copy:

So Tanda waited.

She swept the shipyard. She counted gulls. She named the wood shavings.

There were too many shavings.

That was exactly why they needed names.

### 15. Across the Water

Image beat: The ship at sea in cold mist. Men are tired. The father repairs a rope or spar while the tooth-keeper rests hidden inside his vest.

Read-aloud copy:

Across the water, the ship found wind, fog, and empty stores.

Winter had been hard everywhere.

The men wanted to bring home what their village needed.

### 16. The Barrel

Image beat: On a foreign shore, a boy is discovered hiding inside a barrel aboard the ship with a rope coil. Fish spill comically nearby.

Read-aloud copy:

One morning, they found a boy in a barrel.

He was holding one of their rope coils and smelled so strongly of herring that even the gulls kept their distance.

"A thief," someone said.

### 17. The Easier Choice

Image beat: Crew surrounds the frightened boy. Tanda's father stands between anger and the child, tired and undecided.

Read-aloud copy:

Her father was big enough to frighten him.

He was tired enough to want to.

Taking the rope back would have been easy.

Making the boy afraid would have been easier.

### 18. The Tooth Taps

Image beat: Close on the carved oak tooth-keeper glowing faintly through the seam of his vest. In the glow, a soft memory of Tanda's gap-toothed face.

Read-aloud copy:

Then the tooth-keeper tapped against his chest.

Not loudly.

Just enough.

He remembered Tanda's hands closing around the tooth.

Still mine.

### 19. Eye Level

Image beat: Father kneels all the way down in front of the boy. The crew watches, confused.

Read-aloud copy:

He knelt all the way down, to the boy's eyes.

"What is the rope for?" he asked.

The boy pointed to shore.

### 20. The Small Boat

Image beat: A cracked little boat lies pulled onto stones. A smaller sister stands nearby in patched clothes, watching.

Read-aloud copy:

On the stones lay a little boat with a split side.

Beside it stood the boy's sister, barefoot in the cold.

The rope was not treasure.

It was what he thought might hold their boat together.

### 21. What Mercy Does

Image beat: Father repairs the little boat with his tools. The boy holds a plank. Crew members slowly help. The sister wears spare wool socks.

Read-aloud copy:

Tanda's father took back the rope.

Then he took up his tools.

By dusk, the little boat held together, the boy's sister had socks from the ship's spare wool, and nobody had been hit.

### 22. Slower Than Taking

Image beat: Two shoreside groups trading: nails, wool, fish, rope, repairs. The father watches, still tired but steadier.

Read-aloud copy:

The next day, the two villages traded.

Work for food. Nails for wool. Rope for fish.

It was slower than taking.

It was better.

### 23. First Toothlight

Image beat: Night aboard the ship. The tooth glows inside the opened oak keeper like a small star. The father cups it in both rough hands.

Read-aloud copy:

That night, the tooth glowed inside the little oak keeper like a star that had chosen a small place to live.

Her father did not understand it.

But he knew what had called it forward.

### 24. Homecoming

Image beat: The ship returns. Tanda sees him from the shore but does not run at first. Her anger is still present.

Read-aloud copy:

When the ship came home, Tanda did not run at first.

She had been angry too long to drop it all at once.

Her father came down the plank and knelt in the sand.

### 25. It Carried Me Home

Image beat: Father opens his palm. The carved oak tooth-keeper opens, and the sailcloth-wrapped tooth glows softly inside. Tanda's face changes from guarded to wonder.

Read-aloud copy:

The tooth lay in his palm, bright as dawn.

"It carried me home," he said.

"But not the way I expected."

### 26. What It Held

Image beat: Tanda touches the tooth. The glow becomes a soft vision: herring barrel, cracked boat, boy blinking back tears, sister in socks.

Read-aloud copy:

Tanda touched it.

She smelled herring, heard waves knocking a cracked boat, and saw a boy blink hard because someone had been kind when he expected shouting.

"It remembers," she whispered.

### 27. The Promise Grows

Image beat: Tanda places the tooth in her carved wooden box beside her drawing of a ship, a rope, and two children on opposite shores. Light spreads into her hands.

Read-aloud copy:

"No," said her father. "You remembered first."

Tanda placed the tooth in her wooden box with her drawing: a ship, a rope, two children on opposite shores.

"Then I will remember for others," she said.

### 28. The First Wings

Image beat: The tooth box opens with small flashes: a rooftop, a mouse hole, a song, a seed under dark soil. Warm gold gathers around Tanda's shoulders into the first wings.

Read-aloud copy:

The box answered.

Not with words.

With tiny flashes: a rooftop, a mouse hole, a song, a seed under dark soil.

Around Tanda's shoulders, warm light gathered into wings.

### 29. What She Carries

Image beat: Adult Tanda, visually tied to the existing Tanda reference, with the first tooth pendant near her heart. The sea and stars behind her. Young Tanda's box rests nearby.

Read-aloud copy:

Tanda grew after that, but the promise grew with her.

When children lost what they had outgrown, she could hear the small bright place it belonged.

And the first tooth stayed near her heart, where all good borrowed things should be carried.

### 30. CTA

Image beat: The glowing tooth, the child's drawing, and a soft hint of modern Toothlight without showing a phone as the hero object.

Read-aloud copy:

Your child's tooth is proof they are growing.

Create a Toothlight from the tooth, their words, and the memory you want to keep.

Button: **Create Your Tooth Keepsake**

Secondary line: **Make it part of the Network.**

## Image Generation Workflow

### Pass 1: Character Reference Set

Generate these before any story frames:

1. Young Tanda full body, neutral pose, no wings.
2. Young Tanda expression sheet: proud, annoyed, worried, gap-toothed wonder.
3. Tanda's father full body, shipwright clothes and tools.
4. The boy across the water, neutral and frightened-but-brave expression.
5. Shipyard environment reference.

These five images become the source references for the frame pass.

### Pass 2: Anchor Frames

Generate 6 key frames first and review them before producing the rest:

1. Frame 02, shipyard world.
2. Frame 04, pitch comedy.
3. Frame 10, permission to carry the tooth.
4. Frame 19, father kneels to the boy.
5. Frame 23, first Toothlight.
6. Frame 29, adult Tanda with the first tooth pendant.

If these six match, the rest of the image set is production, not exploration.

### Pass 3: Full Frame Set

Generate remaining frames in batches of 4 to 6. Use the same character references every time. Keep the camera varied: wide establishment, medium family scenes, close-up tooth moments, then the larger magic frames at the end.

### Pass 4: Site Integration

1. Place final images in `public/story-assets/viking-origin/`.
2. Replace the current 10-scene story in `src/data/stories/viking-origin.ts` with this 30-frame version.
3. Update title, description, region, CTA text, and scene IDs.
4. Run the local site and review mobile and desktop story playback.
5. Adjust copy length per frame where text crowds the image.

## Style Recipe For Image Prompts

Use the current 3D references as the authority:

- soft cinematic 3D storybook character design
- warm expressive faces and large readable eyes
- natural textile detail: wool, leather, rope, sailcloth, rough oak
- sea-blue shadows, hearth-amber highlights, gold toothlight
- shallow depth for close emotional beats
- clear mobile-safe composition with subject in the center third or lower third
- no visible text in the images

Use old watercolor art only as a mood ingredient:

- restrained backgrounds
- handmade texture
- less plastic shine
- more softness in skies, fog, and memory visions

Do not let the image system drift into:

- horned-helmet Viking costume
- battle glamour
- adult Tanda appearing before the final frame
- over-sparkled magic in early scenes
- generic fantasy palace or medieval castle settings
- phone/product UI inside the story frames

## Prompt Pattern

Each production prompt should follow this structure:

Subject and action:
Describe the exact characters in the frame and what they are doing.

Location and context:
Describe the shipyard, shore, boat, deck, or firelit room.

Composition:
Describe shot size, camera angle, and mobile-safe framing.

Style:
Current TFN soft cinematic 3D character style, handmade Norse coastal world, warm amber and slate-blue lighting, toothlight as restrained gold glow, no visible text.

Reference instruction:
Use Young Tanda, Tanda's Father, and the established Story 2 style reference. Preserve character faces, clothing, proportions, and hair across frames.

## Locked Spine

The father does not win because of the tooth.

The tooth helps him remember what kind of person he promised to be.

Tanda's magic is not "power from a tooth." It is power from entrusted care:

**She can carry what children outgrow without letting it disappear.**
