# TFN Flagship Trilogy Brief

Status: first approval draft
Date: 2026-05-07

## Direction

Goal: replace the current homepage storybooks with three stronger child-first bedtime stories that share character consistency, storybook image structure, and TFN product meaning without sounding like product copy.

Working trilogy:

1. **Tanda's First Night** - modern network origin.
2. **Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee** - mythic Norse origin / why Tanda is unique.
3. **Perez and the Sealed Window** - Spain / humility and the first outside collector.

Parking for now: Tamil Nadu, Banyan/Punjab, and other embellished anthology candidates. They can return later after the first three prove the format.

## Shared Story Pattern

Each story should include:

- A child protagonist.
- A family anchor: parent, grandparent, godparent, sibling, teacher, or friend.
- A collector whose flaw mirrors the child's problem.
- A tooth ritual that forces a choice.
- A small network echo: one sensory flash of another child's world.
- A Toothlight seed: a note, drawing, phrase, object, name, or photo-like memory the child adds.

The story-world language stays magical. The product-language bridge is Toothlight.

## Story 1: Tanda's First Night

### Job

This story explains why the Network is needed now.

### Core

Tanda is excellent but stretched thin. She has been collecting teeth faster and reading fewer of them. A child adds something unmistakably personal to a tooth, and Tanda realizes the real treasure is not the tooth alone. It is the child's own addition: the word, name, drawing, or memory.

### Child / Family Anchor

Keep the current shape of a modern child with an ordinary, specific interior life. The child should not be precious or literary. They should be practical, funny, and observant.

Candidate child: keep Maren only if the name stops bothering Sathian after the rewrite. Otherwise rename. Avoid gender-ambiguous "AI default" naming.

Family anchor: mother or father who is doing bedtime imperfectly but lovingly. The parent should not be blamed. The story is about how small things disappear even in loving homes.

### Collector Flaw

Tanda mistakes coverage for care. She is doing the job and missing the point.

### Lesson

Asking for help can be responsibility.

### Product Metaphor

The child's added thing is the beginning of a Toothlight.

### Frame Target

18-24 frames.

### Images Needed

- Tanda character reference.
- Child reference.
- Parent/family bedroom reference.
- Tanda's satchel / route map.
- Toothlight first-glow moment.
- Tanda writing or sending the first invitations.
- End frame: Tanda no longer alone.

## Story 2: Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee

### Job

This is the mythic origin. It explains why Tanda is not just a collector, why her magic is different, and why the first tooth fee became a promise rather than a payment.

### Title Options

- **Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee**
- **The First Tooth Fee**
- **The Shipbuilder's Promise**

Recommendation: **Tanda Fae and the Tooth Fee**. It ties her name, myth, and tradition together.

### Setting

Norse/Viking age, coastal settlement. Cold sea, timber, smoke, ship ribs, tools, wool, salt, iron, lanterns. The world should feel handmade.

### Child Protagonist

Tanda as a child. Not yet fairy. Not yet immortal. Curious, stubborn, and attached to her father. She spends time in the shipyard watching him build ships and naming the curled wood shavings that fall from his plane.

### Family Anchor

Her father: a shipbuilder-viking. He helps build the ships that carry people out for trade, resources, and sometimes conflict. He is not framed as a raider hero. He is a maker first, a protector second, and a fighter only when forced.

He knows every plank in a hull because he shaped it. His value is craft, patience, and responsibility.

### Inciting Tooth Moment

Tanda loses a tooth in the shipyard, possibly while trying to bite a strip of dried fish or stubbornly pull a knot tight with her teeth after being told not to.

Her father pays the tooth fee, but instead of treating it as a transaction, he makes a promise:

> "What you outgrow, I will not throw away."

He drills a tiny hole through the tooth, wraps it in a sliver of sailcloth, and wears it on a cord under his tunic before leaving with the ship.

### Child Problem

Tanda is angry that her father leaves. She sees the ship as the thing that takes him away. She wants him to promise not to go, but he cannot.

This is a real child problem: loving someone who has responsibilities outside the home.

### Father Problem

He believes courage means coming back with what the village needs. He has not yet understood that courage may also mean choosing not to take something.

### The Mercy Choice

At sea or in a foreign coastal conflict, the father has a chance to strike, seize, or destroy. The tooth presses against his chest. He remembers Tanda's gap-toothed face and the promise: what a child outgrows is still worth tending.

He shows mercy when force would be easier.

The exact action should be simple and visual, not grand:

- He lowers an axe instead of finishing a defeated enemy.
- He releases a frightened boy hiding under a half-built boat.
- He spares another family's winter stores and takes only what was fairly traded.

Recommendation: he protects another child. It makes the mercy legible to a 5-7-year-old and ties directly to Tanda's future role.

### Magical Transformation

The tooth does not give him victory. It changes what victory means.

When he returns, the tooth has begun to glow. Tanda touches it and sees what it carried: fear, restraint, mercy, the remembered promise. The glow moves into her hands, then her feet, then her back.

Her magic is born from care:

She can carry what children outgrow without letting it disappear.

### Tanda's Powers

Keep them tactile and bounded:

- She can hear the small story sealed in a lost tooth.
- She can follow the faint route of a tooth's belonging.
- She can fly, but only after she accepts that carrying does not mean owning.
- She stays young because her work lives at the edge of childhood. She is not frozen as a child; she becomes the keeper of the moment children grow past.

### Collector / Network Echo

This is before the Network. The echo should be proto-network:

When Tanda first holds the glowing tooth, she feels other lost things across the world very faintly: a song on a roof, a mouse-hole in a wall, a seed under soil. She does not understand them yet.

This plants the future without explaining it.

### Lesson

Courage is not just what you can take or survive. Courage is what you choose to protect.

Mercy is strength under pressure.

### Product Metaphor

The tooth fee becomes a promise to preserve the child's outgrown thing. A Toothlight is the modern version of that promise.

### Humor / Child Chuckle

Keep the Norse story from becoming too solemn:

- Young Tanda insists she is old enough to help build a ship and immediately glues her sleeve to a plank with pine pitch.
- She names every wood shaving. Her father tells her there are too many. She says that is exactly why they need names.
- A goat tries to eat the sailcloth wrapping.
- Her father is a master shipbuilder but terrible at braiding small cords.

### Frame Target

24-32 frames. This one can be longer. It is the keystone.

### Beat Outline

1. Cold shore, half-built ship, child Tanda in the shipyard.
2. Father shaping a plank; Tanda watches and asks too many questions.
3. Tanda tries to help and gets pine pitch on herself.
4. Establish father as maker: he knows ships by sound and touch.
5. Tanda's tooth is loose; she refuses to admit it.
6. Tooth comes out during a stubborn child-task.
7. Father explains tooth fee: a lost tooth has value because growing has value.
8. Tanda wants a coin; father gives the coin but also asks to keep the tooth.
9. He makes the promise: what you outgrow, I will not throw away.
10. He wraps the tooth in sailcloth and hangs it near his heart.
11. News: the ship must leave.
12. Tanda is angry. She wants the promise to mean he stays.
13. Father says promises do not stop storms; they guide you through them.
14. Ship leaves. Tanda watches from shore.
15. At sea: rough weather, crew tension, need for resources.
16. They reach another shore / conflict.
17. Father sees a chance to take by force.
18. He finds or notices another child afraid.
19. Tooth presses against his chest; Tanda's memory flashes.
20. Mercy choice: he lowers the weapon / protects the child / chooses fair trade.
21. The tooth glows for the first time.
22. He returns home changed, not triumphant.
23. Tanda is still angry until she sees he kept the tooth.
24. She touches it and sees the mercy memory.
25. The toothlight spreads into her hands.
26. She hears other lost teeth faintly across the world.
27. Wings or first flight moment, strange and tender.
28. Father tells her: some things are carried so they can return.
29. Tanda understands her work: carry what children outgrow.
30. Final image: Tanda older/unchanged by time, first tooth pendant glowing, looking toward future routes.

### AI-Tell Guardrails

Avoid:

- "not because... because..."
- over-explaining the moral
- battle glory
- destiny language
- "ancient, timeless, magical" piles
- making the father a perfect saint

Use:

- shipbuilding detail
- child irritation
- small funny specificity
- one clean mercy scene
- the promise repeated only twice

## Story 3: Perez and the Sealed Window

### Job

This story introduces the first non-fairy collector and proves that Tanda is not building a fairy empire. She is building a network of sovereign collectors who keep their own rituals.

Tanda's challenge is interpersonal. She cannot simply arrive in Madrid and "fix" Perez. She has to learn his tradition, respect his pride, and understand that his way of collecting is not a weaker version of hers. Perez has something to teach her too: ceremony, memory, theatrical care, and the dignity of doing one route beautifully for 130 years.

### Relationship Engine

Tanda and Perez should have friction from the first scene together.

Tanda arrives because Madrid's light has flickered on her map three nights in a row. She assumes the problem is operational: missed route, sealed window, modern building. She comes with tools, route charts, and a slightly too-helpful attitude.

Perez finds this insulting.

He does not need fairy rescue. He has survived Madrid changing around him for 130 years. He knows drainpipes, balconies, shutters, kitchens, old walls, new elevators, and which grandmothers leave crumbs by the window. He is not behind. He is precise.

Except one tooth is still under Sofia's pillow at dawn.

The relationship is not mentor/mentee in one direction. It is two experts learning the limits of their expertise:

- Tanda teaches Perez that asking for help protects the child, not the ego.
- Perez teaches Tanda that a network must adapt to local rituals instead of smoothing them into one system.

### Child / Family Anchor

Child: Sofia in Madrid.

Family anchor: her abuelo or abuela, who remembers Perez from childhood and takes the ritual seriously. The grandparent gives the story warmth, continuity, and a reason Perez matters beyond "mouse collects tooth."

Sofia's ordinary problem should mirror Perez:

She made a mistake and hid it. Good candidate: she ruined part of a school drawing, folded it under the bed, and pretended it was lost. The tooth note/drawing can become her way of telling the truth.

### Collector Flaw

Perez's flaw is not incompetence. It is pride as self-protection.

He has a secret drawer of uncollected teeth or unresolved attempts. Not many. Enough to haunt him. He calls them "pending matters," not mistakes.

### Tanda's Flaw In This Story

Tanda over-corrects from Story 1. After learning to ask for help, she now thinks connection means building procedures. She arrives with a plan before she has listened.

Perez forces her to slow down and observe the ritual in Madrid: the glass of water, the pillow, the balcony routes, the tiny doors, the family memory around Perez.

### Ritual Problem

The old route fails because Sofia's family lives in a renovated building with sealed windows, tight door sweeps, and smooth walls with no mouse passage. The world has changed around Perez's ritual.

He tries everything. Too much, actually:

- drainpipe
- balcony line
- dumbwaiter shaft that is no longer a dumbwaiter
- a heroic but humiliating attempt involving a pastry string

This should be funny before it becomes painful.

### The Cost

Sofia wakes up and the tooth is still there.

For a child, that is the emotional stake. The magic forgot her.

For Perez, it is the impossible thing: his record is broken.

For Tanda, it is a warning: the Network cannot be a map of glowing dots. It has to solve real local problems with real collectors.

### Tanda / Perez Turning Point

Tanda catches Perez trying to hide the missed tooth in his drawer.

Wrong version: she scolds him.

Better version: she recognizes the drawer because she has her own version of it: unread teeth from Story 1. She does not say "I understand" too neatly. She simply opens her satchel and shows him one tooth she failed to read.

Two experts. Two private shames.

That is the moment they become peers.

### Earned Resolution

They solve it together, but Perez must lead the final collection.

Tanda can shrink, lift, or glow, but Perez knows the building. The grandparent's old story gives them the missing route: not the window, not the door, but the old tile crack near the radiator / pantry pipe / courtyard vent.

Perez reaches Sofia's tooth. Sofia has left a note or drawing admitting her hidden mistake. Perez reads it. He does not fix the drawing for her. He leaves a small sign that her truth was received.

At breakfast, Sofia chooses to show the damaged drawing to her grandparent/parent.

### Lesson

Humility is not saying you are bad. Humility is telling the truth before the mistake gets lonely.

Mistakes do not break you. Hiding them makes them heavier.

### Product Metaphor

The Toothlight is not perfect because the photo/drawing/note is polished. It is valuable because the child added the true thing.

### Network Echo

When Sofia's tooth joins the chain, Perez hears a shipbuilder's hammer from Tanda's old story and a child's rooftop song from a future Kkachi story. Tanda smells warm biscuit tin and sea salt at the same time. Neither explains it.

### Humor / Child Chuckle

- Perez's route notebook has headings like "Balconies: Reliable," "Cats: Unreliable," and "Modern Windows: Rude."
- He keeps emergency crumbs in a vest pocket and calls them "professional provisions."
- Tanda is baffled by how many rules Perez has for crossing one street.
- Perez calls elevators "vertical rooms with opinions."
- A pastry string rescue attempt leaves him dusted in sugar and pretending it was intentional.

### Frame Target

22-28 frames.

### Beat Outline

1. Madrid at night, warm windows, old streets, tiny mouse door.
2. Perez preparing for his route with theatrical precision.
3. Sofia loses her tooth after a day of hiding a spoiled drawing.
4. Abuela/abuelo tells her where to put the tooth and remembers Perez.
5. Sofia leaves tooth with a small folded drawing/note.
6. Tanda sees Madrid flicker on her map.
7. Tanda arrives in Madrid with a route idea.
8. Perez is offended before she finishes one sentence.
9. He demonstrates his system with pride.
10. First attempt at Sofia's building fails: sealed window.
11. Second attempt fails: drainpipe / smooth wall / wrong vent.
12. Tanda offers help too quickly; Perez refuses.
13. Comic route sequence; Perez gets dusted in sugar or tangled.
14. Dawn approaches. The tooth is still under the pillow.
15. Sofia wakes. The tooth is still there. Her face falls.
16. Perez hides the miss in his drawer.
17. Tanda finds him and sees the drawer.
18. Tanda shows one of her own unread teeth.
19. Silence. Peer recognition.
20. Grandparent detail reveals an old route / local clue.
21. Perez leads; Tanda assists without taking over.
22. Perez reaches the tooth and reads Sofia's note/drawing.
23. He leaves a gift/sign that honors the truth, not perfection.
24. Sofia chooses to show the spoiled drawing.
25. Tooth joins the chain; sea salt, biscuit warmth, faint rooftop song.
26. Perez writes Tanda a very long letter agreeing to "limited coordination."
27. End image: Tanda and Perez on a Madrid rooftop, not merged, connected.
