# The Polytheistic Test

## What Saraswati and Lakshmi ask of AI, crypto, and power

“Why does Saraswati carry music?” one of my seven-year-old daughters asked me.

I was born Hindu. It took my children to make me study what I had inherited. Their questions are direct: Why does Lakshmi sit on a flower? Why do different gods hold different things? Why does knowledge need music?

I gave them a simple answer. Saraswati represents learning, speech, memory, music, and discernment. Lakshmi represents prosperity, abundance, good fortune, and flourishing. One asks what you understand. The other asks what you do with what you have.

That answer became a test I now use on technology.

Every technology should pass two tests: does it deepen human understanding, and does it create durable flourishing?

This is not a complete map of Hinduism. Hindu traditions are plural, regional, and full of overlapping meanings. What stays with me is the refusal to make one figure carry every human need. Knowledge, prosperity, protection, destruction, and devotion can have different faces. Power is divided so we can inspect it.

## What if AI is polytheistic?

I encountered a modern version of this frame through Balaji Srinivasan and the Network School: “AI is polytheistic, not monotheistic.”

There may never be one all-knowing model. There are many models, built by different groups, with different strengths, limitations, values, and patrons. One may be better at code. Another at writing, images, research, or reasoning through a long document. None deserves to become an oracle.

Polytheism, as I am using it here, is not a claim that software is divine. It is a refusal to collapse every kind of power into one authority. The better question is not simply “Which AI wins?” It is: what kind of power does this model offer—and what is missing from it?

## The Saraswati test

- Does this deepen human capability?
- Does it make important claims easier to verify?
- Can an ordinary person understand its rules and limitations?

AI can make intelligence easier to access. It can also make confident nonsense easier to produce. A model can help a child learn, help a founder write software, or help a fraudster manufacture evidence. Faster output does not pass the Saraswati test. Better judgment might.

> **Image:** `public/media/flagship-lakshmi.png`  
> **Suggested caption:** The Lakshmi test: value that survives the promotion cycle.

## The Lakshmi test

- Does this create durable flourishing?
- Does it solve a real problem people will return to?
- Can the people carrying the risk share the upside?

Crypto creates a different temptation. It can make ownership and settlement programmable. It can also build a faster casino and call the activity adoption. A rising token price does not pass the Lakshmi test. I want to know what value remains, who receives it, and who carried the risk.

## Testing the ledger

I used the tests on the Solana Observatory, a dashboard I built to ask whether Solana is reliable, used, economically meaningful, and becoming real financial infrastructure.

The Saraswati side forced every number to carry its definition, source, freshness, and limitation. A metric nobody can understand is decoration. The Lakshmi side forced a harder question: does the activity create durable value, or are we mistaking motion for progress?

A public ledger can preserve rules, balances, and receipts so independent people can inspect them. But a ledger is not truth itself. It can prove what was recorded and which rules executed. It cannot automatically prove that a house exists, a person is honest, or an AI answer is correct.

## Other places I am testing this

The Tooth Fairy Network asks whether a childhood ritual can become a durable memory without turning family affection into speculation. The Agent Allowance Lab asks whether software can receive a useful budget without receiving an unlimited wallet. My site assistant asks whether research and memory can become easier to question without turning an AI answer into authority.

None proves the framework. They are where I try to break it.

## Where this metaphor could break

AI is not Saraswati. Crypto is not Lakshmi. Both contain knowledge, capital, power, error, and incentives. The weak seams deserve their own explorations: what happens when crypto communities become gated communities, when access becomes status, and when a protocol’s openness stops at the front door?

I do not need my daughters to inherit my conclusions. I want them to inherit better questions.

**What do we know? Who flourishes? What can the ledger actually prove?**
