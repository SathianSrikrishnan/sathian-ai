## 1. The Spine — Tanda's Vision

The world is globalizing. Families move. Languages blend. Local traditions dissolve into a shared global default. The tooth fairy used to be a hundred different characters — a mouse in Madrid, a magpie in Seoul, a hyena in Addis Ababa, a peacock in Tamil Nadu. Now she's becoming one generic fairy in a tutu, holding a wand, everywhere. The richness is flattening.

Tanda saw this happening. She's the North American Tooth Fairy — competent, exhausted, running a crew of four (Flicker, Brume, Clink, and herself) across six time zones. She's good at her job. But good at her job means seeing things other operators miss: tooth collections declining in regions where families have moved. Korean children in Toronto don't sing to the magpie anymore. Ethiopian children in London don't call the hyena. The old rituals are fading, and when they fade, the teeth lose their glow — the stories sealed inside go dim.

Tanda's idea is not to standardize. It's the opposite: **preserve every local tradition by connecting them.** The network doesn't replace the magpie with a fairy. The network means the magpie can call the fairy when a Korean kid moves to Canada, and the fairy can call the magpie when a Canadian kid's grandmother in Seoul wants the tooth to go home.

**This is the product metaphor (never spoken aloud in any story):** each tradition is a local currency. The network is the protocol that lets them interoperate — without erasing any of them. The chain of teeth is the shared ledger. Each tooth remembers which tradition handled it, which child lost it, and which family celebrated it.

## 2. Tanda — Character Deep Dive

**Name:** Tanda
**Role:** Founder and coordinator of the Tooth Fairy Network. De facto CEO, but she'd hate that word.
**Appearance:** Not glamorous. Practical bun, satchel overflowing with route scrolls, wings that wilt when she's tired (which is always). She looks like a tired project manager who happens to be able to fly.
**Personality:** Visionary builder. She sees the systems underneath, not just the teeth on top. Fierce in a quiet way — she doesn't shout, she just keeps going until the thing she's building exists. She's been doing this for centuries.
**Flaws:**
- Pushes too hard. Asks others to join the network before they're ready.
- Privately doubts whether she's building something that will last or something that will break under its own weight.
- Has a blind spot for the emotional cost of connection — she sees the architecture but sometimes misses the feelings of the operators she's recruiting.
**Her team:** Flicker (fast, anxious), Brume (reads teeth, cries easily), Clink (new, clumsy, asks the smart questions). They cover NA + Western Europe. That's all.
**Her arc:** Lonely competence → reaching out → rejection → persistence → the chain proves her right → the network connects.
**Key distinction from prior series-bible framing:** Tanda is NOT "the builder reaching out" as a personality trait. She's the visionary who sees homogenization coming and decides to do something about it. The reaching out is strategic, not social. She builds the network because the alternative is extinction of local traditions.

## 3. The Taxonomy — "Breeds of Ritual-Keepers"

The network is a **federation of regional camps**, each with its own practices, appearance, and lineage. They are NOT variations of one character. They are distinct traditions that share the function of "keeper of a child's first transition."

Think of it like breeds of dogs. A golden retriever and a Siberian husky are both dogs — same underlying species, same deep-rooted purpose (companionship, loyalty). But they look different, behave differently, evolved in different climates, excel at different tasks. You wouldn't mistake one for the other. That's the fairy taxonomy.

### Fairy camps (fairy-type collectors)
These are winged, magical, moonlight-operating keepers. They're what most Western children think of when they hear "tooth fairy."
- **Tanda's crew (NA + Western Europe):** The youngest major operation. 4 fairies, stretched thin, high-efficiency but no slack.
- **Anna Bogle (Ireland):** The OLDEST fairy operator. Leprechaun lineage. Has been doing this since before Tanda was born. Skeptical of the network because she's seen cooperation fail before. Has her own missing tooth (traded it to a child centuries ago) — she's gap-toothed and proud of it.
- **Finnish Fairy (Hammaskeiju):** Paired eternally with the Tooth Troll (Hammaspeikko). They're adversaries but not enemies. The fairy wants to place teeth on the chain. The troll wants to keep them in his perfectly organized cave. Their conflict is the only real antagonist dynamic in the network.

### Mouse camps (mouse-type collectors)
Small, theatrical, ground-level operators. They don't fly — they sprint, climb, and squeeze.
- **Ratón Pérez (Spain + Latin America):** Lives in a biscuit tin on Calle del Arenal, Madrid. 130 years old. Suave, proud, fast. Red velvet pouch. Spectacles. His collection runs are action sequences — rooftop sprints, balcony leaps, drainpipe slides. He reads teeth by tapping them against his spectacles like a tuning fork.
- **French La Petite Souris, South American El Ratón:** Related lineages but different operators. Wall-card tier. The Pérez family tree has branches.

### Sacred animal camps (animal-type collectors)
Animals with cultural-spiritual weight. They carry more gravitas than the fairies — they're older, heavier, less playful. The teeth they handle are more solemn, more rooted in landscape and ancestry.
- **Kkachi the Korean Magpie:** The network's messenger. Fast, loud, social. Sings constantly — Korean folk songs mixed with melodies from every culture she's visited. Reads teeth by weaving them into her nest, where the stories unfold as dreams. She's attracted to shiny things; her nest is full of tiny treasures from around the world.
- **Ethiopian Hyena:** Looks terrifying, sounds terrifying, is the gentlest operator in the network. Children throw their teeth into the dark; the hyena takes them and returns at dawn with a gift. Nobody has ever seen him do it. The courage exchange — child faces fear, hyena validates bravery.
- **Mayil the Indian Peacock (NEW):** Sacred bird of Kartikeya in Hindu mythology. Iridescent, impossibly beautiful. Reads teeth by fanning them with her tail — each eye-feather reflects a different moment from the child's life. The visual metaphor is overwhelm: when Mayil fans her tail, the child sees their own memories scattered like prisms. She operates at twilight — not night, not day. The peacock's call at dusk is how Tamil children know it's time.
- **Anka the Amazonian Jaguar (NEW):** Night hunter whose eyes reflect the teeth she collects. Silent, strong, precise. Inspired by Tupi-Guarani oral tradition (with disclaimer). She operates in deep rainforest canopy — children leave teeth on a leaf by the forest edge. She watches from the branches until the family goes inside. Her method: she breathes on the tooth, and the jungle breath seals the story. The visual: two golden eyes in the dark, then gone.

### Tree-spirit camps (rooted collectors)
These don't move. The children come to them. They're the oldest operators, older than any animal or fairy — they predate human memory.
- **Hazara the Banyan Tree-Spirit (NEW):** A presence in the roots of an ancient banyan. Teeth planted in the earth around Hazara's roots become part of the tree's growth — each tooth is a root node, connecting to the teeth before and after. The permanence metaphor is the spine of this camp: the banyan outlives everyone. Children visit the tree, place the tooth in a hollow between roots, and press their hand to the bark. Hazara whispers back through the leaves. What does Hazara look like? A shimmer between the aerial roots. A face that might be bark. A figure that dissolves when you look directly at it.

### Uncategorized
- **Hammaspeikko (Finnish Tooth Troll):** Not a collector — a hoarder. He keeps teeth in his cave, perfectly organized, because he's afraid that sharing them means losing them. He's the kid at school who won't share his toys. He doesn't join in Season 1. He gives one test tooth to the Beaver's archive. It glows brighter than any tooth in his cave. He'll think about it. (Season 2.)
- **Babylonian Tooth Worm:** 3,800 years old. Archaic. The oldest documented tradition. Wall-card tier for now.

## 5. The Chain of Teeth — Mechanics

### What exists (from series-bible, ~70% done)
- A tooth with its story intact glows faintly gold.
- A tooth that's been lost, forgotten, or left too long goes dull.
- A tooth on the chain — connected to the tooth before it and after it — glows brightest.
- The Beaver's archive has amber veins connecting every tooth through his lodge.
- When a tooth is placed, the veins pulse once with light. The chain grows.
- Light flows tooth-to-tooth, linking the child to the one before and after in their lineage.
- "You are now part of a chain that stretches back centuries."

### What's missing (~30%, drafted here)
**How the chain prevents loss:**
The chain is not a backup — it's a living memory. When a tooth is on the chain, its story plays for anyone who touches it. Remove it, and the story fades in 100 years. Leave it, and the story lasts as long as the chain lasts. The chain has been growing since the first tooth was placed by an operator long before Tanda. Nobody knows who placed the first one. The chain IS the network's proof that this works.

**The age-18 reveal:**
When a child reaches 18, the chain unlocks its full view for them. They see their tooth alongside every tooth in their lineage — parents, grandparents, great-grandparents if they participated. Each tooth plays its sealed story. The 18-year-old sees themselves at 5, giggling while the tooth wiggled. They see their mother at 6, in a different country, with a different operator. The financial value is visible now (the SOL that was deposited has compounded). But the emotional reveal is the story.

**The philosophical moment (to be seeded in Tanda's story):**
Tanda explains to a skeptic (Anna Bogle, who's seen networks fail before): *"A tooth alone is a thing that was lost. A tooth on the chain is a thing that was kept."* Anna's counter: *"Things get lost on chains too."* Tanda's response: *"Not this one. This one remembers."*

Tanda's recruitment journey. Each episode introduces one new camp joining the network.

| Ep | Character recruited | The tension | How it resolves |

| 2 | Anna Bogle (Ireland) | Tanda visits the oldest fairy. Anna has seen this idea before — a network of operators — and it collapsed. | 8 | Finland (conflict episode) | The Troll won't let the Fairy join the network. "If she joins, she'll give away MY teeth." The Fairy wants to join but won't abandon the Troll — they've been paired for centuries. | A race through the aurora. Not for a tooth — for a principle. If the fairy wins, she joins and the Troll gets to audit the chain (he'll find it well-organized; it'll appeal to his hoarding instinct). If the Troll wins, both stay independent. **Season 1 finale beat:** All 9 operators' first teeth glow on the chain simultaneously. A pulse of gold light travels from tooth to tooth, around the world, in the order they were placed. The map lights up. The network is connected. Clink, watching from the treehouse, says: "That's a lot of teeth." Tanda says: "That's a lot of kids."
